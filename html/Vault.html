1958 Gibson Moderne

The mythical third sibling to the Flying V and Explorer. While Gibson filed a design patent for this futuristic, asymmetrical guitar in 1958, no authenticated 1950s original has ever publicly surfaced. It remains the ultimate "ghost" guitar in collector circles, though Gibson later released official reissues in the 1980s and 2010s.

1958–1959 Gibson Korina Explorer

Considered too radical for players in the late 1950s, this angular guitar was a commercial flop. Gibson shipped only around 22 original units between 1958 and 1959, making authentic examples some of the rarest and most expensive production solid-bodies ever built.

1950 Fender Broadcaster

Designed as a solid-body counterpart to the hollow-body White Falcon, the White Penguin featured gold-plated hardware, Filter'Tron pickups, and lavish sparkle binding. Because it was custom-order only and very expensive, fewer than 50 originals were ever produced.

1955–1962 Gretsch White Penguin

While more common than the Explorer (with roughly 1,200 to 1,700 made), the original Sunburst Les Paul is widely considered the "Holy Grail" of electric guitars. Its combination of a carved flame maple top, Brazilian rosewood fretboard, and original PAF humbuckers makes surviving examples worth hundreds of thousands of dollars.

1958–1960 Gibson Les Paul Standard ("Burst")

The "Holy Grail" of electric guitars, prized for its sunburst flame maple top and PAF humbuckers, with roughly 1,200–1,700 made.

Brian May’s "Red Special"

Hand-built in the early 1960s by Queen guitarist Brian May and his father using wood from an 18th-century fireplace mantel, motorcycle valve springs, and mother-of-pearl buttons. There is literally only one original in existence, making it irreplaceable.

1954 Fender Stratocaster #0001

Famously owned by Pink Floyd’s David Gilmour (and sold at auction for $1.815 million), this white-and-gold guitar is one of the earliest pre-production Stratocaster prototypes ever assembled.
